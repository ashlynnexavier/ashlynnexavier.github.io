Five-year-old me thought her new hearing aids were the coolest thing ever. Not for any intellectual reason; they just looked like something out of Star Wars. As I grew older, I learned just how complex, ever-evolving, and—most importantly—life-changing they were. Two little devices helped open up so many opportunities that never would have been possible otherwise. I wanted to help build the next generation of such technology.

That is exactly what I get to do in my current role as a junior systems and electrical engineer in the Neural Bypass and Brain-Computer Interface Lab at the Feinstein Institutes for Medical Research, under PI Chad Bouton. There, alongside the engineering team, I support experiments aimed at restoring movement and sensation in patients affected by stroke and spinal cord injury. My work has included developing data processing and computer vision pipelines, designing 3D-printed components for functional task assessments, soldering and testing circuits used in data collection, and more.

Prior to my work there, while earning my B.S. in Biomedical Engineering at the University of Rochester, I designed and ran experiments for two computational neuroscience labs and spent a summer at Boston University conducting materials science research with a startup developing an electrochemical biosensor for multiple sclerosis.

Outside of engineering, I enjoy running, reading, music, and art, usually accompanied by my cat, Callie. Feel free to reach out and chat!
